STRATA · Architecture & Interior Design — Toronto

Spaces shaped by light, material, and restraint.

A five-person studio in Toronto designing residences, workplaces, and rooms worth standing still in — across Southern Ontario and occasionally beyond.

01 — Manifesto

We don’t decorate rooms. We resolve the tension between how a space is used and how it feels to stand inside it — one material decision at a time.

That is the whole method. Everything below — the studies, the drawings, the numbers — is what it looks like when it is followed properly.

03 — The Drawing

Drawn before it was built.

Every project here starts as a plan, and the plan is where the argument is won or lost. Ours is shown at the scale it was worked at — then dragged against the photograph of the finished room, so you can see exactly which line became which wall.

Ridgeline Residence — ground plan, working drawing 1:200

Drawing over photograph — drag the handle

PhotographLine study

The handle is a real slider: drag it, or focus it and use the arrow keys. The line study is the same photograph reduced to its drawing — mass, glazing, and the path the light takes.

04 — Our Approach

A slow, deliberate sequence.

Four moves, no shortcuts. Each one ends with something you can hold: a site report, a spatial narrative, a set of drawings, a building.

01

Discover

Site analysis, client vision, constraints mapping before a single line is drawn. You leave with a written brief you agreed to.

02

Concept

Spatial narrative, massing studies, and a material direction — argued in person, with physical samples on the table.

03

Detail

Technical drawings, material specification, engineering coordination — the stage that decides whether the finished room looks like the drawing.

04

Deliver

Construction oversight, styling, and the final reveal — by the same people who drew it. Nothing is handed off.

05 — The Studio

“I started STRATA after a decade designing for other people’s visions.”

Here, every project starts with the site and the client’s actual life in it — not a mood board. The land tells you what it can hold; the people tell you what it should feel like. Our job is to make those two answers agree.

The team is small on purpose: five people, and every project touched by the same hands from first sketch to final walkthrough. Nothing is handed off, nothing gets lost between departments, and nothing leaves the studio we would not want to live with ourselves.

Nadia Ostrow
